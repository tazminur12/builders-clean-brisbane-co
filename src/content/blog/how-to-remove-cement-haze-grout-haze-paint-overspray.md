**Short answer:** identify the surface first, then the residue, then test a small hidden area. Cement and grout haze on glazed porcelain usually comes off with a dedicated haze remover and a non-scratch pad. Paint overspray on glass comes off with a wet, fresh scraper blade held flat. Natural stone, terrazzo and polished concrete need non-acidic methods, because acids etch them permanently. If you are not sure what the surface is, stop and ask.

This guide is for owners and builders who want to try it first. It is also the method our crew follows.

## Why the residue happens

**Cement and grout haze** is a thin film of cement left when a tiler wipes grout off the face of a tile. It dries cloudy and is hard to see until the sun hits the floor.

**Paint overspray** is a fine mist of paint that lands on glass, frames, tiles and floors when walls and ceilings are sprayed or rolled.

**Silicone smears** come from sealing showers, windows and benchtops. Cured silicone is clear, so it shows up as streaks and dull patches.

**Plaster residue** is the white film left on walls, skirting and floors after sanding.

## What not to use

- **Acids on stone, terrazzo or polished concrete.** Brick acid, vinegar and many "tile cleaners" dissolve cement, and they also dissolve calcium-based stone and polished concrete. The damage is a permanent dull etch.
- **Abrasive pads on glass, stainless or high-gloss surfaces.** Green scourers and melamine sponges scratch.
- **Dry scraping.** A blade on dry glass drags grit across it. Always wet the glass first.
- **Solvents on unknown surfaces.** Some solvents soften plastics, powder coat and sealers.
- **High-pressure water on soft surfaces.** It can blast grout out of joints and etch render.

## Step by step, by surface

### Glazed porcelain and ceramic tile
1. Sweep and vacuum to remove grit.
2. Test a haze remover suited to porcelain in a hidden corner.
3. Apply, agitate with a white non-scratch pad, and do not let it dry.
4. Rinse with clean water and wipe dry. Repeat if needed.
5. Check in daylight from a low angle.

### Glass and window frames
1. Check for coatings (low-E, tinted, self-cleaning). Some cannot be scraped.
2. Wet the glass well with soapy water.
3. Use a fresh blade held flat at a low angle, pushing in one direction only.
4. Rinse and squeegee. Never scrape frames or powder coat.
5. For silicone, soften with a silicone remover first.

### Natural stone (marble, limestone, travertine) and terrazzo
1. Do not use acid. Test a pH-neutral or alkaline cleaner suited to stone.
2. Work gently with a soft pad and plenty of water.
3. Stubborn haze on stone is a job for a professional; the risk of etching is high.

### Polished concrete
1. Avoid acids completely.
2. Use a pH-suitable concrete cleaner and a soft pad or a machine with a fine pad.
3. Rinse well and buff dry. Our [floor cleaning after construction](/services/floor-cleaning-after-construction-brisbane/) covers this.

### Paint on tiles and floors
1. Fresh water-based paint usually softens with warm soapy water.
2. Dried paint on glazed tile can be lifted with a plastic scraper.
3. On timber, stone or concrete, test any paint remover first, or call a pro.

## Preventing residue next time

If you are a builder or renovator, the cheapest residue is the one that never dries. A few habits on site make the final clean much faster:

- Ask the tiler to do a final haze wipe once the grout has firmed, before they leave.
- Mask glass, frames and benchtops before spray painting, and remove masking promptly.
- Remove manufacturer stickers and film from glass early, before the Brisbane sun bakes the adhesive on.
- Keep a bucket and sponge near the work area so splashes are wiped while wet.
- Book a [rough clean](/services/builders-rough-clean-brisbane/) between trades so residue does not get walked through the house.

None of this replaces a final clean, but it can take hours off it.
