**Short answer:** in Queensland, the builder is responsible for cleaning a new home only to the extent the building contract says. Most builders do a basic clean before handover, and most owners book their own full clean before moving in. If you want a specific standard, it has to be written into the contract, or you book it yourself.

That is the answer we give homeowners most weeks. Here is how it works in practice, and what to do about it.

## What the contract usually says

Residential building contracts in Queensland deal with the building work: the plans, the specifications, the price, variations, practical completion and defects. Cleaning is usually mentioned, if at all, as a line in the inclusions list or the specification, such as "builder's clean on completion" or "site cleaned and rubbish removed".

What that line rarely says is **how clean**. A "builder's clean" can mean anything from rubbish removed and floors swept, to a full interior clean by a professional crew. Without a defined standard, the builder has met the contract when they have done what they consider a builder's clean.

When you read your contract, look for:

- The inclusions list or specification: is there a line about cleaning?
- Any wording on the standard: "professional clean", "ready for occupation" or similar
- Whether windows, cupboard interiors and light fittings are mentioned
- Whether external areas (driveway, paths, alfresco) are included

If none of this is there, assume the builder will do a basic clean and plan for a full clean yourself.

## What the handover guidance does and doesn't cover

At handover, the focus is on the build: is the work complete, does it match the plans, and what defects need fixing. Handover guidance and pre-handover inspections are about workmanship, not cleanliness. A dusty cupboard or a sticker on a window is unlikely to be treated as a building defect in the way a cracked tile or a leaking shower would be.

That is why we suggest homeowners treat cleaning separately: note anything obvious at the inspection (paint on glass, cement on tiles, damage hidden by dirt), but do not expect the handover process to produce a clean home.

## Builder's clean vs owner's clean

Here is the difference we usually see when we arrive at a new home after the builder's clean:

| Area | Typical builder's clean | Full new home clean |
|---|---|---|
| Floors | Swept or vacuumed | Vacuumed, mopped and buffed to suit the surface |
| Benchtops | Wiped | Cleaned, residue and stickers removed |
| Cupboards and drawers | Often not done | Wiped out inside, including runners |
| Windows and tracks | Sometimes glass only | Glass, frames, tracks and sills |
| Light fittings and fans | Rarely | Dusted top to bottom |
| Grout and tiles | Mopped | Haze removed where needed |
| Photo report | No | Yes |

Neither is "wrong": they are different jobs. The builder's clean gets the home ready to hand over. The owner's clean gets it ready to live in. Our [new home builders clean](/services/new-home-builders-clean-brisbane/) is built for that second job.

## How to write cleaning into a contract

If you are still at the contract stage, you can ask for cleaning to be defined. Some owners ask the builder to include a professional clean by a specialist cleaner, to a written checklist, before handover. Others prefer to leave it out and book their own clean, so they control the standard and the timing.

If you want it included, ask for:

- A named standard, for example "professional builders final clean to a room-by-room checklist"
- Cupboard and drawer interiors, window tracks and light fittings specifically mentioned
- Removal of stickers, paint spots and grout haze
- Timing: after the last trade and before the inspection

You can attach our [builders clean checklist](/builders-clean-checklist/) as a reference. Talk to your builder or a building consultant before changing contract wording; this article is general information, not legal advice.

## When to book the owner's clean

The best time is **after handover and before move-in**. Trades have finished, so dust is not being created any more, and the home is empty, so every cupboard and floor can be done properly.

1. Book as soon as you have a handover date. Good slots go early in busy estates.
2. Ask the builder when the last trade will be on site. A returning electrician or painter can undo a clean.
3. Leave at least one day between the clean and the removalist if you can.
4. If you cannot be there, arrange a key or lockbox. We send a photo report when we finish.

If you are buying in one of Brisbane's growth estates, such as [North Lakes](/areas/north-lakes/), handover dates can move at short notice. Pick a cleaner who will move with the date.

## What if the home was left in a mess?

If the builder's clean was clearly below what your contract promised, raise it in writing with the builder, with photos. If the contract does not define cleaning, it is usually quicker and cheaper to book a clean yourself than to argue about it. Paint on glass, cement on tiles or damage you find during the clean is different: photograph it and report it to the builder as a possible defect.

## The bottom line

The builder cleans what the contract says, and most contracts say very little. Plan for your own full clean, book it for the day after handover, and get a quote that lists what is included.
