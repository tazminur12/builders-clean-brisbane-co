**Short answer:** walk the home in daylight with a written list, room by room, and check the build first and the cleanliness second. Note every defect in writing with a photo, give the list to your builder, and book your own full clean for after handover. This checklist covers what to look at in a Queensland new home.

This is general guidance from a Brisbane cleaning crew who sees new homes every week. It is not a building inspection. For a full technical inspection, use a licensed building inspector.

## Before you go

- Ask for the inspection in daylight, with power and water connected.
- Bring a torch, painter's tape or sticky dots to mark defects, your phone for photos, and a copy of the plans and colour selections.
- Ask whether the builder's clean has been done. Heavy dust can hide scratches, chips and paint misses.
- Allow at least two hours for a family home.

## What an inspector looks at

A pre-handover inspection is about workmanship: is the home built to the plans and the contract, and are the finishes acceptable? Inspectors look at alignment, finishes, function and damage. They do not judge whether the home is clean, but they will struggle to see defects under construction dust.

## Room-by-room checks

### Kitchen
- Benchtops: chips, scratches, joins and silicone along the splashback
- Cabinetry: doors aligned, drawers run smoothly and close softly, handles tight
- Appliances: the right models, film removed, no dents
- Sink and tapware: no leaks, hot and cold both work

### Bathrooms and ensuites
- Tiles: cracked or chipped tiles, lippage between tiles, grout gaps
- Shower: screen sealed, door closes, falls drain to the waste
- Vanity: drawers and doors aligned, basin sealed
- Toilet: firm to the floor, flushes properly
- Exhaust fan works

### Living areas and bedrooms
- Walls and ceilings: paint misses, patches, cracks, nail pops
- Doors: open and close without catching, latches engage, stops fitted
- Wardrobes: shelves level, sliding doors run smoothly
- Power points and switches: all work, plates straight

### Windows and doors
- Glass: scratches, chips or paint on the glass (check at an angle)
- Frames: dents, scratches, missing seals
- Sliding doors and windows open, close and lock
- Screens fitted where they should be

### Floors
- Tiles and grout: cracks, uneven tiles, haze left on the surface
- Timber and hybrid: scratches, gaps, lifting edges
- Polished concrete: chips and stains
- Carpet: joins, stains and loose edges

### Outside
- Driveway, paths and alfresco: cracks, cement splashes, falls away from the house
- Render and cladding: cracks, paint misses
- Gutters and downpipes connected

## Cleanliness: note it, but don't fight it

Cleaning items are worth noting at the inspection, especially anything that may be damage: paint on glass, cement on tiles, stains on benchtops. But dust in cupboards, stickers and smears are usually not treated as building defects. The practical approach: accept that you will book a clean, and use our [builders clean checklist](/builders-clean-checklist/) to make sure it covers everything.

## Questions to ask your builder

1. Has the builder's clean been done, and what did it include?
2. Will any trades return after handover? If so, when?
3. How do I report defects after handover, and how long do I have?
4. Where are the manuals and warranties for appliances and systems?
5. Who do I contact for maintenance questions in the first months?

## Queensland conditions to keep in mind

Brisbane's humidity and storm season affect new homes in ways that are easy to miss at an inspection. Check that wet areas have working exhaust fans, that wardrobes and cupboards are not closed up with damp packaging inside, and that doors and windows close properly, because timber can move with the weather. Look at where water will go in a storm: paths and driveways should fall away from the house, and downpipes should be connected.

After handover, open the home up when you can. A closed new home in a humid week can develop a musty smell or mould spots in wardrobes and wet areas before you have moved in. A clean that wipes out cupboards and wardrobes, and leaves the home aired where access allows, helps.

## After the inspection

1. Send the defects list in writing with photos, and keep a copy.
2. Confirm the handover date and the date of any return visits by trades.
3. Book your clean for after the last trade, ideally the day after handover. In growth estates like [Yarrabilba](/areas/yarrabilba/), book early.
4. Take dated photos of the home at handover for your records.

For builders, the same logic applies in reverse: a [handover clean](/services/builders-handover-clean-brisbane/) timed to the inspection takes cleaning off the client's list, so the inspection focuses on the build.
