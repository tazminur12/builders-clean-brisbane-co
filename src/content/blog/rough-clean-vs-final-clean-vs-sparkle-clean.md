**Short answer:** a **rough clean** happens during the build, between trades, and clears debris and dust so the next trade can work. A **final clean** happens after the last trade and takes the home to handover standard, every surface top to bottom. A **sparkle clean** is the detailed last pass before a sale, an open home or move-in. They are three different jobs at three different times.

## Stage 1: the rough clean

**When:** during construction, usually after plaster and before painting, or before tiling and floor coverings.

**What it removes:** offcuts, packaging, cardboard and plastic; plaster and cement dust on floors and sills; debris in corridors and walkways.

**What it does not do:** detail work. Nobody wipes inside cupboards at this stage because trades are still coming through.

**Why it matters:** painters get a dust-free surface, which means a better finish. Floors are clear for the next trade. Dust does not spread into finished rooms. And the final clean starts from a clean base, which saves time and money later. See our [builders rough clean](/services/builders-rough-clean-brisbane/) page.

## Stage 2: the final clean

**When:** after the last trade has left and before the handover or practical completion inspection.

**What it removes:** construction dust from every surface, ceilings to skirting; stickers and light residue from glass; dust inside cupboards, drawers and pantries; grout haze and marks in wet areas; dust on fans, light fittings and vents. Floors are cleaned to suit the surface.

**What it does not include:** heavy cement haze, paint overspray and high-level exterior windows are usually quoted separately.

**Why it matters:** this is the clean that takes the home from a construction site to a finished home. If it is rushed or happens before the last trade, cleaning items end up on the defects list. See our [builders final clean](/services/builders-final-clean-brisbane/) page.

## Stage 3: the sparkle clean (or handover clean)

**When:** just before the keys change hands: the inspection, a sale, an open home or move-in day.

**What it adds:** detail work beyond the final clean: window tracks and sills detailed, cupboard interiors and hinges, light switches and door frames, glass and mirrors streak-free, appliance interiors where in scope, and a last buff of the floors.

**Handover vs sparkle:** a [handover clean](/services/builders-handover-clean-brisbane/) is timed to the practical completion inspection, with a supervisor walk and a photo report for the builder. A [sparkle clean](/services/sparkle-clean-brisbane/) is aimed at owners, sellers and agents who want the home presentation-ready.

## Side by side

| | Rough clean | Final clean | Sparkle clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| When | Between trades | After the last trade | Before keys, sale or move-in |
| Who books it | Builder | Builder or owner | Owner, agent or builder |
| Debris and packaging | Yes | Yes | n/a |
| Dust on every surface | Floors and sills | Everything | Everything, second pass |
| Inside cupboards | No | Yes | Yes, detailed |
| Glass | No | Yes, inside | Streak-free, tracks detailed |
| Photo report | Short note | Yes | Yes |

## How staging saves money

It sounds counter-intuitive, but two cleans can cost less than one. When a site has not had a rough clean, the final clean crew spends hours shifting heavy dust and debris before they can start the detail. On estate builds in places like [Ripley](/areas/ripley/), where homes are handed over in batches, a rough clean between trades also stops dust tracking between homes.

For homeowners, staging is simpler: if the builder has done a final clean, you may only need a sparkle clean before move-in. If the builder's clean was basic, book a full [new home clean](/services/new-home-builders-clean-brisbane/).

## How long each stage takes

As a rough guide for a single home: a rough clean is usually a few hours to a day, depending on how much debris has built up. A final clean is usually a full day for a crew, longer for large two-storey homes with a lot of glass. A sparkle clean on a home that has already had a final clean is usually shorter, because it is detail work rather than dust removal. Your quote confirms the time on site for your home.

## Common mistakes with staging

The two mistakes we see most are booking the final clean before the last trade has finished, and skipping the rough clean on a big job to save money. The first means paying twice, because the returning trade brings dust back in. The second usually means the final clean takes longer and costs more than a rough clean would have.

## Which one do you need?

- **Builder, mid-build, next trade waiting:** rough clean.
- **Builder, last trade finished, inspection booked:** final clean or handover clean.
- **Owner, keys received, moving in soon:** new home clean or sparkle clean.
- **Owner selling or leasing a new or renovated home:** sparkle clean.
